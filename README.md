# Choosing an innovation platform for a university

A vendor selection I led for a university's innovation program in 2023: 19 platforms, three rounds, one recommendation.

**19** platforms sourced · **10** after desk research · **5** compared in depth · **1** recommended

## Context

The program needed one place to collect and sort the ideas feeding the university's innovation funnel. It had three goals: build an innovation culture, protect intellectual property and manage assets, and evaluate and develop ideas as businesses. With the university's commercialization office, we set the requirements for the tool, from idea submission and feedback to asset inventory and licensing. I led the evaluation, working with another product manager.

## How we chose

**Sourcing.** We listed 19 platforms from a software review marketplace, referrals from colleagues and web searches.

**Round 1, desk research.** From the end of January into early February, we checked each tool's public information against the goals. That left 10.

**Round 2, calls and demos.** From February 13 to 28, the other product manager and I split the discovery calls and demos, around six questions: does it meet the goals and requirements, are the user and admin interfaces easy to use, what can it report, what can it integrate with, is there a trial, and is there an accessibility report. We added two tools the university already had, because they were available to us and could potentially be used. Vendor A was the only outside vendor that met all nine criteria. One internal tool did too, but it needed time and money.

**Round 3, analysis.** Three vendors and the two internal tools went side by side on 12 criteria, from the goals and user roles to trial terms, timeline and support.

## The decision

I recommended Vendor A. Among the reasons: it could support the program's goals and requirements, its admin and user interfaces were easy to use, its reporting could be customized, it had integration potential with the university's other systems, it had an accessibility report, and it was flexible enough to change with the program. I coordinated the procurement and was the vendor's point of contact. It was bought, and I handed it off to operations.

## Design decisions

- **Compare against what we already had.** The internal tools could be built to fit, but they'd need development first. One was estimated at 6 to 12 months for its first stage, depending on scope, against vendors that worked out of the box.
- **An accessibility report isn't full support.** Vendor A had one, and we noted that having a report doesn't mean it supports everything.
- **Goals before features.** Every round checked the tools against the program's goals, and the finalists were scored against them.
